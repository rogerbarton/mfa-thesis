## 1. The project in short

A *diskursive Arbeit* on how generative AI is reshaping human thinking — reasoning, decision-making, learning, memory. The submitted abstract asks: which forms of human cognition become obsolete, what significance of human cognition remains, are new forms emerging.

Two hypotheses are already on record:

1. Storey's notion of **cognitive and intent debt**, developed for software engineering, extends beyond that field to individual thought processes. The cognition demanded of us is shifting from calculating answers to finding them — we become reviewers.
2. Against Shaw & Nave's predicted **"cognitive surrender"** — adopting AI outputs with minimal scrutiny — we can, in relevant cases, resist.

The second is the more valuable of the two, because it is the only claim made so far that could turn out to be wrong. **[session]**

Kick-off guidance worth keeping visible:

## 5. The four threads

### Intent — losing track of what you wanted
Storey's intent debt generalised beyond software. The answer is cheap; knowing what you were after is not. This is the only one of the four that names something genuinely **new** — the other three are older problems in newer clothes. **[session]** It already has a material double in the studio: *raw wood, used to build something, already partially processed, lost our intention.*
**Cost [session]:** it leans hardest on the professional authority currently being hidden, and can slide into a software essay before you notice.
*Sources: [Storey (2026)](./References/From%20Technical%20Debt%20to%20Cognitive%20and%20Intent%20Debt%20_%20Queue.pdf) · [Notion — Master Thesis](https://app.notion.com/p/36f00818ef8580af9e41eb5c39c14bc9) (Hypothesis; "lose track of your intent") · [Notion — Bad Ragartz Plan](https://app.notion.com/p/3a300818ef8580729ad9fc70897807d9) (wood notes)*

### Free will and optimisation
Optimisation sidechains the decision. Decision exhaustion, willpower as a finite resource, the paradox that living in the moment is both the height of free will and its surrender — releasing yourself from memory and from your original intention, submitting to feeling as it meets the environment. Clear's *Atomic Habits* is the reference already in the notes: willpower is limited, so it must be spent governing the autopilot — our free will is weak, and should be used to control the will we have already lost. See §10 for how to handle that claim safely.
**Cost [session]:** free will is a 2'500-year argument that cannot be won in 40'000 characters and will eat the whole text if allowed. The observation underneath — *optimisation removes choices you never knew you had* — is sharp and needs no metaphysics. Keep the observation, bracket the term.
*Sources: [Notion — Master Thesis](https://app.notion.com/p/36f00818ef8580af9e41eb5c39c14bc9) (opening free-will notes; the Atomic Habits paragraph; the "live in the moment" paradox; BR paragraph on overthinking and burnout) · [Clear (2018)](#popular-self-help--as-evidence-not-as-authority)*

### Memory and its externalisation
What is lost when a memory is projected into paper, and again into a machine. The firmest ground and the best company: Plato's *Phaedrus* (Theuth and Thamus, writing as *pharmakon* — a recipe for reminding, not for remembering), the art of memory and the method of loci (the "pinner door" note is exactly this), Benjamin, Stalder. Carries the strongest material analogy: the metal-spraying technique is literally transfer from a source into a new form, with remnants of the old kept visible.
**Cost [session]:** well-trodden. You have to arrive somewhere Plato didn't.
**Note [session]:** this is the thread least dependent on professional authority — it stands on the literature and the studio alone. If the background stays hidden, this is the safest spine.
*Sources: [Notion — Master Thesis](https://app.notion.com/p/36f00818ef8580af9e41eb5c39c14bc9) (Nick's notes, 8.6; "What about memory?") · [Notion — Bad Ragartz Plan](https://app.notion.com/p/3a300818ef8580729ad9fc70897807d9) (metal transferred from a source, remnants embedded)*

### The reviewer condition
We stop producing answers and start approving them. Shaw & Nave's cognitive surrender, and the cases where it can still be resisted. Closest to the abstract already submitted, so it is what the mentor already has in mind, and it holds the falsifiable claim.
**Cost [session]:** the most journalistic of the four; dates fastest.
*Sources: [Shaw & Nave (2026)](./References/Shaw%20&%20Nave%20(2026)%20-%20Thinking,%20Fast,%20Slow,%20and%20Artificial%20(PsyArXiv).pdf) · [Harari (2017)](./References/Harari%20-%20New%20Perspectives%20Quarterly%20-%202017%20-%20Dataism%20Is%20Our%20New%20God.pdf) · [Notion — Master Thesis](https://app.notion.com/p/36f00818ef8580af9e41eb5c39c14bc9) (Hypothesis; "Calculating the answer is only one way of finding the answer")*

### The question underneath all four **[session]**
> **What happens to a process when its substrate changes?**

Judgment into approval. Intention into prompt. Memory into paper into machine. Wood into metal. It may not be necessary to choose one thread — it may be enough to name this question and let the four become movements.

**Do not state the sculptural analogy in the text.** Let it operate. An explicit metaphor kills the reader's own reading; an operating one does the work quietly.

## 6. The exploration method — four probes **[session]**

One probe a week through September. Each ~2'000 characters. **One thread each, and deliberately a different form each.** No revising, no rescuing a bad one.

This tests spine and form simultaneously and cheaply. At 8'000 characters, most of the material survives into the thesis, so the exploration is not a detour.

In week five, read them back and ask **not** which argument was strongest, but **which one got away from you.** Choose the one that surprised you, not the one you can already defend. The probe you can defend is the one you have already finished thinking about.

*Sources: working session, 23 Aug 2026*

## 7. Form options being tested

- **Argued essay, light apparatus.** One continuous voice, references where they earn it. Most legible to a jury of four; least distinctive.
- **Braided registers.** A research voice and a first-person voice alternating, visibly distinct on the page. The friction between them does the arguing.
- **Dated journal with a methodology preface.** Entries in sequence, contradictions preserved, framed up front. Closest to the June sketch. Requires the journal to have actually started — see §8.
- **Form enacts the argument.** The writing performs the drift, interruption, or surrender it describes. Highest ceiling; real risk of reading as gimmick if the frame is not airtight.

Felix's argument for the experimental end: the situation with AI is volatile and will not stabilise soon, so a form that can leave a contradiction standing is *more* honest than one that must resolve it. That is the defence to give the jury if the risky form wins.

## 8. The episode problem

**The gap:** as of 23 August 2026, no lived episodes of Roger's own cognitive surrender have been collected. The text lives or dies on two or three concrete, slightly unflattering ones. Felix asked for this explicitly in June — *start a journal of my own experiences* — and it has not started.

**Retroactive mining — do this first. [session]** Months of AI chat histories, Notion logs and commit history are a timestamped archive of his own cognition. Read them back looking for the moment he stopped checking. The dates are real, the drift is visible, and reading yourself as a stranger gives the writing exactly the distance it needs. This produces material immediately rather than in November.

**Forward capture — must be near-frictionless [session]** on a one-day-a-week schedule. Not a daily journal. A note on the phone, triggered by a feeling rather than a schedule:

- the moment you accept an output without reading it properly
- the moment you can't recall what you were originally after
- the moment you feel **relief**

Record: date, what you were doing, what you did. **No interpretation in the entry.** Interpretation is the writing day's job; mixing the two ruins both.

**If the dated-journal form is a live option, the journal has to be running now.** September cannot be honestly retrofitted in October.

## 9. Two positions to state openly **[session]**

### The AI-use declaration
Proofreading for coherence, exploring concepts and summarising reading is precisely the behaviour the thesis is about. That is not a problem — it is free primary data. But it must be declared in the methodology note, specifically and without softening. A thesis arguing about cognitive surrender while quietly outsourcing coherence is fragile. One that states exactly what was used, where, and what the writer noticed happening to himself is very hard to attack, and more interesting.

**One restriction:** read [Storey](./References/From%20Technical%20Debt%20to%20Cognitive%20and%20Intent%20Debt%20_%20Queue.pdf) and [Shaw & Nave](./References/Shaw%20&%20Nave%20(2026)%20-%20Thinking,%20Fast,%20Slow,%20and%20Artificial%20(PsyArXiv).pdf) unsummarised. The hypothesis is that surrender can be resisted in relevant cases. Those two papers are the relevant case.

### The background
Hiding the software background entirely costs more than it saves. The Storey material will read as tourism — a fine artist explaining software engineering from outside — and the jury will quietly wonder how that paper was read so comfortably.

Biography is not needed. **One line, positioned as method rather than CV** — *I spent years inside a field where this happened first, which is why I trust the observation and distrust the conclusion* — buys the authority and costs nothing.

If it stays fully hidden: choose the memory thread, and let the fluency leak out as **precision** rather than as biography.

### Wikipedia — as maps, not as authorities

Felix's instruction was to use the wiki to *pick a few classic theories*. Treat these as route-finders: follow each down to the primary theory and cite that. A jury will accept an encyclopaedia entry as a way of choosing what to read, never as the source of a claim.

- [**Thought**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought) — already reference [1] in the submitted abstract, as "Theories of thinking". The shortlist to choose from: which two or three theories of thought the text will actually stand on.
- [**Knowledge**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge) — justified true belief and the Gettier problem live here. **[session]** This one is directly load-bearing for the reviewer condition (§5): if you approve an output that happens to be correct, without having checked it, do you *know* it? That is a very old question and it lands exactly on the thesis. Probably the most useful of the five.
- [**Dataism**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dataism) — the term as Harari and Brooks put it into circulation; useful for seeing who else has used it and how, given how much weight Harari's arc carries in the argument.
- [**Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_Deus:_A_Brief_History_of_Tomorrow) — go straight to the reception and criticism. **[session]** Harari is widely read and widely disputed; the thesis is stronger for knowing where he is challenged before a jury member raises it.
- [**Education Index**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_Index) — the UNDP measure inside the Human Development Index, built from mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling.

**On the Education Index [session].** Worth seeing what it actually is before leaning on it: it counts *duration*, not what anyone learned. An index that substitutes years-in-a-building for knowledge is itself an instance of the thesis's own problem — a measurable stand-in quietly taking the place of the thing it stood for. If AI changes how learning happens, this index will not register it at all. That silence may be more useful to you than the numbers. Primary source for the actual figures: [UNDP Human Development Reports](https://hdr.undp.org/data-center/human-development-index); a good critical overview at [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/human-development-index).

### Popular self-help — as evidence, not as authority

- **James Clear (2018).** *Atomic Habits: An Easy & Proven Way to Build Good Habits & Break Bad Ones.* Avery / Penguin Random House.
- **Mark Manson (2016).** *The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F\*ck: A Counterintuitive Approach to Living a Good Life.* HarperOne.

Both already appear in the notes as "AH, MM" and behind the line about the *rise in self-help books and podcasts*. Clear is the one that touches the free-will thread directly (§5): willpower is finite, so spend it governing the autopilot. Manson sits closer to the other half of the same notes — overthinking, the burden of too much choice, burnout as a generational condition.

**How to use them [session].** Not as authority for a claim about cognition. The finite-willpower model behind Clear (Baumeister's ego depletion) is genuinely contested — a 23-lab registered replication reported an effect close to zero, and the argument is still running in the literature. Cite it as established and a jury member who reads psychology will take the paragraph apart.

Use them instead as **evidence of the moment**. Millions of people buying books about how to manage their own attention and intention is itself a data point for this thesis. Self-help is where a culture goes to admit what it can no longer do by itself — and the boom arrives at precisely the moment the machines offer to do it for us. That reading is safe, original, and closer to what the thesis is actually about than any claim either book makes.

*On the replication problem: [Hagger et al. (2016), multi-lab registered replication](https://nature.berkeley.edu/garbelottoat/wp-content/uploads/hagger-chatzisarantis-2016.pdf) · [The Past, Present, and Future of Ego Depletion, Social Psychology (2024)](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1864-9335/a000398) · [Self-control and limited willpower: current status (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39278166/)*

## 11. Advisor guidance on record

**Felix (8 June 2026).** Treat it as an unanswerable question. Clarify the problem, move through different perspectives, question your own approach and experiences. Note how it changes your approach to material. Distinguish learning / thinking / creativity — learning is a subset of thinking; learning is fitting new information into an existing structure (and therefore where bias lives); thinking is making sense of experience and weighing deliberately against intuition; creativity is finding new ways of thinking. The AI situation is volatile and won't stabilise soon, which is what makes experimental writing appropriate: no answer required, contradictions can stand, and it uses the background well. Possible framing: *how AI changes our thinking in the context of learning* — intellectual, not physical. Start a journal of your own experiences. And: what about memory?

**Nick (8 June 2026).** Harari's arc — secularism (the answer is in God), humanism (the answer is inside you, after a lot of thinking), dataism (the answer is in the data). Outsourcing memory changes memory; some is lost in projecting it into another memory type, paper or digital. Not a new struggle.

**Earlier (1 June 2026).** Pick something you would do either way. Self-reflective: why is it relevant to *you*? Document the research process — a narrative of the research. The thesis is a supporting document to the work. Start with a question or hypothesis and make the formulation interesting: the question is a condensed statement that opens a discussion and then condenses into new questions. It need not be hyper-academic with references. For an experimental text, write an accompanying text describing the methodology and intention — what does the experimental method uniquely allow you to do?
